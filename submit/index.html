1  You were right about the Indexing API

Google's own documentation, updated 16 July 2026:

“The Indexing API can only be used to crawl pages with either JobPosting or BroadcastEvent embedded in a VideoObject.”

We called it for all 72 URLs on 27 August and every call returned “accepted”. They were accepted and then ignored. Sitemap plus manual submission is the only route for pages like ours, exactly as you said.

2  But indexing is not stuck — and this is the finding that changes the plan

I reported 12 of 72 this morning. It is now 31 of 72. Googlebot has been working through the site continuously since launch.

31 pages crawled between 2026-08-27 13:10 and 2026-08-28 16:26 UTC — a steady 1.14 pages per hour. 6 on day one, 25 on day two. At that rate the remaining 41 arrive on their own in roughly 1.6 days.

And discovery is provably not the problem: I checked every one of the 41 uncrawled URLs against the link graph — all 41 are already linked from a page Google has crawled AND indexed, and all 72 are in a sitemap Google re-downloaded today with zero errors. Googlebot knows about every one of them. The constraint is crawl rate on a two-day-old domain, not discovery, not a block, not a technical defect.

That is why the submission list below is worth doing at the top and not worth grinding to the bottom: for most of these pages you would be paying a manual submission for something arriving free within 48 hours.

3  What the investigation killed

37 agents across seven independent lenses, each finding then attacked by a refuter and a separate check for whether Google documents the mechanism at all. Thirteen findings were killed, one survived. The most seductive one is worth showing you, because it is exactly the kind of thing that reads as insight and is wrong.

KILLED: “hreflang in-degree drives crawling — discovery travels sideways along the hreflang mesh.”
The statistic was real and reproducible (pages inside an hreflang cluster crawled at 60%, orphans at 8%, p = 0.0003 even after controlling for geo). It was still wrong. Google's own docs say a crawlable link is “an <a> HTML element with an href attribute” — hreflang is <link rel="alternate"> in the head, and the hreflang documentation never mentions crawling or discovery once. Worse, the two never-crawled URLs with the highest hreflang in-degree are /se/ and /dk/, which beats the in-degree of every page that was crawled — the exact opposite of the theory. The correlation was geo scheduling wearing a costume.
SURVIVED, but weakly, and I am flagging it as weak: the cluster-vs-orphan crawl split is genuinely there and I cannot fully explain it. It is not a reason to act. Re-measure on 30 August; if the orphans get picked up on the same schedule, it was nothing.

4  Three real defects — none of them the indexing story, all worth fixing

  1. hreflang is split across two canonical-key groups. The page types added in the 72-page expansion (hub, product form, does it work) emit a correct 12-URL mesh. The original types do not. The reviews cluster has nine pages across nine geos and not one names any other — each points only at itself. /fr/pharmacie/ emits a 7-URL retailer mesh that excludes /de/apotheke/ and /uk/boots/. 45 of 72 pages are affected, and all 13 DE and UK pages from the original build are hreflang singletons. This does not block indexing — it decides which country's page Google shows — but reviews and retailer are 31% of everything this portfolio has ever earned. Fix: put the DE and UK pages into the same canonical-key groups as their foreign equivalents.
  2. www.glp3-b.shop serves the entire site at HTTP 200 with no redirect. I verified it: /, /uk/ and /de/apotheke/ all answer 200 on the www host. Canonicals correctly point at the apex so the index will not split, but it doubles the crawlable URL space on a site whose only measured constraint is crawl rate. One redirect line in the Worker.
  3. http://glp3-b.shop/ got indexed as its own URL. Google ignored the /uk/ canonical on the redirect stub and indexed the stub, where it has taken 1 of the site's 2 lifetime clicks and 6 impressions on the only query the site ranks for. The http host now returns a proper 301, so this should self-correct on recrawl — worth watching, not worth acting on.

5  The submission order — all 41, most valuable first

Ranked by page type × geo, both measured, not guessed. Page type comes from the 1,579 clicks this portfolio has earned (hub 42.8% → reviews 18.5% → retailer 12.5% → legitimacy 8.1% → form 5.8% → does-it-work 3.2% → buy 1.5%). Geo comes from the 2,404 clicks earned across osanix, ozem-plus, RetaFit and Wego6 in the last 180 days (UK 37.5% · DE 28.2% · PL 14.1% · IT 10.1% · SE 3.5% · FR 3.4% · DK 3.0% · FI 0.1% · NO 0.0%). The two missing hubs go first because every other page in those two countries sits behind them.

Where to do it: Search Console → URL Inspection → paste the URL → Request Indexing. This is manual only — the URL Inspection API cannot request indexing, so it cannot be scripted.
How many per day: Google does not publish the number. Its documentation says only “there is a daily limit of inspection requests for each property”. Around 10 a day is the commonly observed ceiling — that is community observation, not documentation, and I am labelling it as such. The days below are batches of 10 on that assumption.
My honest advice: do Day 1 and stop. Then look again on 30 August. On the measured crawl rate most of Days 2–5 will already be indexed by the time you reach them.
