1  You were right about Write.as — and it was worse than one bad pick

Write.as publishes the answer in its own pricing table. I never opened it; I fetched write.as/matt/ and measured the links. That page belongs to Matt Baer, the founder of Write.as, on a staff account — so of course the links were dofollow.

From Write.as's own pricing table:
Do-follow links  |  Free: —  |  Pro: ✔  |  Team: ✔
Free plan status  |  "Closed for now"

Dofollow is a $6/month paid feature, and free registration is closed — the VA could not have opened an account even if we wanted one. Two independent kills.

The same mistake had run through the whole list. I checked five platforms by reading a page that belonged to the platform itself: googleblog.blogspot.com (Google's own blog), blog.weebly.com, news.livejournal.com, notion.so/blog and write.as/matt. A company's own page is whitelisted, hand-built and always clean. It tells you nothing about the page a fresh account produces. So I threw the list away and measured the only corpus that cannot lie: every backlink our VAs have ever published.

The relationship is inverted. Wego6 has the most usable backlinks we have ever built — 28 — and earns 73 clicks. Osanix has 6 and earns 1,039. It is enough to say that no free-platform link we have ever placed shows up in the revenue, and that spending the VA's hours here has a poor record.

60 of 72 pages are "URL is unknown to Google" — never crawled, not once. Denmark is at zero. That is the whole story of the last two days: we are not being out-ranked, we are not being seen. A page Google has never fetched cannot rank no matter how many links point at it.

The 12 that are in are doing fine on their own, with essentially no backlinks: average position 5.5, and the brand term glp3-b already sits at 6.8 on 8 impressions.
What the 4 GLP3-B links the VA has already published are worth: Google Sites → /uk/, Tumblr → /de/ and Blogger → /pl/ are all live, indexable and dofollow. The LiveJournal one is dead weight — noindex, nofollow, noarchive and the URL is rewritten through a redirect. Three of four landed; one was spent on a platform I should not have approved.

5  What I would do next

  1. Point wave 2 at the 60 uncrawled pages, not the hubs. The hubs are already indexed — a link there is spent on a page Google has. The one thing these platforms demonstrably do is give Googlebot a fresh path to a URL it has not seen, and that is exactly what is missing right now.
  2. Use four platforms only: Google Sites, Tumblr, Blogger, Telegraph. Every other name on the list either serves noindex, nofollows the link, deletes the post, or charges for dofollow.
  3. Re-place the LiveJournal link on one of the four, pointed at a Danish page — Denmark has zero indexed pages of seven.
  4. Verify every placement after the VA reports it. One Strikingly post was reported done and has no link in it at all; seven WordPress.com posts were deleted after publication and nobody knew. A placement is done when the checker says so, not when the VA does.
  5. Stop expecting juice from this channel. On our own numbers it has never produced any. Treat it as a crawl path, keep it cheap, and judge it on pages crawled rather than on links built.
